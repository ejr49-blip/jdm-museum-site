Exhibit plaques

Four defining JDM artifacts from the 1990s.

1998

Toyota Supra Mk4

A twin-turbo legend that defined 90s performance with power, styling, and pop culture fame.

  • Engineering: 3.0L 2JZ-GTE inline-six, twin-turbo, rear-wheel drive.
  • Cultural impact: Immortalized by street racing media and tuning culture.
  • Collector value: High demand for clean examples and period-correct builds.

The Supra is the archetype of JDM street legend: powerful, widely imitated, and instantly recognizable.

1999

Nissan Skyline GT-R R34

A technological showcase with ATTESA E-TS AWD, an RB26DETT engine, and a futuristic cockpit focus.

  • Engineering: advanced AWD traction, limited-slip differential, over 276 hp in Japan.
  • Cultural impact: A hero car in games, films, and motorsport communities.
  • Collector value: Valued for rarity, performance, and iconic design.

The R34 represents Japanese engineering ambition at its peak, built to dominate both road and racetrack.

1995

Mazda RX-7 FD

A lightweight, balanced driving machine centered around a rotary engine and sculpted aerodynamic form.

  • Engineering: 1.3L twin-rotor rotary, sequential twin-turbo, near 50/50 weight balance.
  • Cultural impact: Cult following for its handling precision and unique engine character.
  • Collector value: Appreciated for engineering bravery and distinct aesthetics.

The RX-7 turned technical risk into art, showing that performance could be elegant and unconventional.

1991

Honda NSX

A supercar built for everyday use, blending race-derived technology with Japanese reliability.

  • Engineering: aluminum monocoque, mid-mounted V6, VTEC, and near-perfect visibility.
  • Cultural impact: Redefined what a Japanese performance car could be in the exotic sports car market.
  • Collector value: Celebrated for pioneering engineering and usability.

The NSX is a design manifesto: precise, intelligent, and built to change the expectations of a generation.
